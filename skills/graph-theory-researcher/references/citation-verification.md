# Citation Verification

## Source Hierarchy

Publisher/journal/DOI record; arXiv; author or university page; professional mathematical organization; other web source.

Prefer the highest available source in this order. A search-result snippet is a discovery aid, not a verified record. Use a secondary source only with an explicit limitation when a primary or authoritative record cannot be reached.

## Required Metadata

Books: author, title, edition if relevant, publisher, year.

Papers: authors, title, venue, year, volume/issue/pages when available, DOI or stable identifier.

Websites: author/institution, page title, complete URL, access date, relevance.

Preserve names, title, capitalization where meaningful, identifier, and publication status as shown by the verified source. Mark metadata that is genuinely unavailable; never guess it.

## Cross-Checks

Open the target, compare title/authors/year, confirm the content supports the recommendation, and record the verification source.

For books, cross-check the author, title, edition when relevant, publisher, and year against the opened record. For papers, cross-check the authors, title, venue, year, volume, issue, pages, and DOI or stable identifier when available. For websites, cross-check the author or institution, page title, complete URL, access date, and relevance.

Resolve arXiv-versus-published differences, corrected versions, and duplicate records. Check that the cited theorem or topic actually appears in the source and that its hypotheses match the use being made of it. Record the stable target and verification date.

## Rejection Rules

Omit or quarantine unreachable, conflicting, fabricated, or incomplete entries. Never invent metadata.

Do not silently merge conflicting records. Label quarantined entries as unverified, state the conflict or missing field, and keep them out of the verified bibliography and any dependent claims.

## Attribution Labels

Source-paper citation; independently found learning resource; analyst recommendation.

Attach one label to each recommendation so the user can distinguish the paper's own bibliography from later discovery and analyst judgment.
